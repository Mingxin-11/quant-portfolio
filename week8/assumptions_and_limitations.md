## Part 1 — Limitations

Reading that every limit refers to: over the 105 test months the data neither show the long-only maximum-Sharpe rule beating 1/N nor show the two to be equal. M = 60 minus 1/N: +0.005 [−0.428, +0.439]. M = 120 minus 1/N: +0.106 [−0.348, +0.539]. M = 120 minus M = 60: +0.101 [−0.091, +0.292]. Zero is inside every interval (the wider of the formula and bootstrap intervals is quoted), and true gaps of roughly ±0.4 to ±0.5 Sharpe units stay open.

Each limit says what is limited, how big it is, which way it moves the reported number, and what the reading can still bear. None adds a conclusion.

**1. Universe.** Four US stocks (two banks, a staples firm, a software firm), picked in September 2026 as present-day large companies. In practice the optimiser held three of them: BAC almost never appears in the weights chart (7.6). Four assets is the favourable case for an optimiser: fewer parameters to estimate, and naive diversification works less well (the draft, p. 3). The draft's six datasets have 3 to 24 assets (Table 2, p. 41; only its simulations go up to 50), and its Proposition 1 (p. 20) says 1/N is more likely to win as the number of assets rises and the estimation window shortens. So the result does not carry to larger universes, where the draft expects the optimiser to do worse. Direction: our test is easier for the optimiser than the paper's.

**2. Period.** One path of 105 months. Sixty of them (2019–2023) were studied in Weeks 2–6; the other 45 (2018, and 2024-01 to 2026-09) were not. In the sub-period check (8.2) the gaps against 1/N change sign: M = 60 is +0.224 [−0.333, +0.781] in 2018–2023 and −0.552 [−1.147, +0.003] in 2024–2026; M = 120 is +0.334 [−0.210, +0.877] and −0.392 [−1.170, +0.301]. The differences (−0.776 and −0.726) have intervals that contain zero (upper ends +0.018 and +0.184). The later period's intervals are wide because it has only 33 test months, not because the estimation windows were short (M is the same in both). The data cannot say whether the sign change is more than noise, and with one path nothing here says how the result looks in other decades. Direction: unknown.

**3. Costs.** Trades happen at month-end prices with no spreads, costs or taxes. Average monthly turnover (8.2), target to target / with drift: M = 60 0.1308 / 0.1151; M = 120 0.0882 / 0.0751; 1/N 0.0000 / 0.0366. Costs fall most on the strategy that trades most, so ignoring them makes the optimisers look better against 1/N: the reported gaps are too high. At an assumed 25 basis points per unit of turnover the annual drag is about 0.35%, 0.23% and 0.11% (about 0.020, 0.013 and 0.006 Sharpe units; mentor arithmetic), and no gap moves by more than about 0.014, against standard errors of 0.221 and 0.226. Costs cannot hide an optimiser win; they could only deepen 1/N's lead, by a small amount.

**4. Optimiser class.** One rule: each month, the exact long-only maximum-Sharpe weights from the sample mean and covariance of the previous M months (60 and 120), with no shrinkage. The test says nothing about the unconstrained mean-variance rule (never run out of sample here), or about shrinkage, minimum-variance or turnover-limited rules. The draft finds that estimation error wipes out the gains of the unconstrained rule in its datasets (p. 31), that constraints and shrinkage help only moderately (p. 3), and that its constrained rule, mv-c, is below 1/N in four of six datasets and above it in two, significantly in one (p. 15). Our rule is the same non-negativity constraint on the same plug-in problem, but the identity with mv-c is not shown (the draft's Section 7.7, p. 29, does not fit a non-negativity constraint alone; the published version is unchecked). Direction: none for our numbers; the limit is what they cover.

**5. Hindsight and MSFT dependence.** The four stocks were picked in 2026 for documented reasons (sector structure, clean data, MSFT as the Week 2 fallback) with their history knowable. The 2019–2023 returns, and MSFT's large weight in the Week 6 maximum-Sharpe portfolio, were studied before the test was fixed, and the test window contains those years. An optimiser that loads on stocks that happened to do well makes the reported gap too high; the size cannot be measured inside this design, and a run without MSFT would change the universe, so it was not added. The buy-and-hold check is a different matter: buy-and-hold 1/N has Sharpe 0.7886 against 0.7790 for rebalanced 1/N, +0.010 [−0.165, +0.184], so the two versions of the benchmark cannot be told apart. "Momentum" is a cause this design cannot test.

**6. Precision.** The intervals are wide (see the reading above). The smallest detectable gaps are 0.619 (M = 60), 0.633 (M = 120) and 0.274 (M = 120 minus M = 60): a true gap below about 0.6 against 1/N could not have been found, and an interval with zero inside is not evidence of equality. The formula and the paired bootstrap both assume independent months; their agreement (bootstrap SE over formula SE 0.977 to 0.994) does not test that. The dependence check (8.3) found 2 of 72 autocorrelations outside ±0.191; the circular block intervals (lengths 6 and 3) were no wider than the locked ones except one, by under 0.015, and their SEs were 0.74 to 1.02 times the locked formula SEs. That is no sign of dependence, not proof of independence: a dependence of φ ≈ 0.2, at the edge of what the test flags, doubles the false-alarm rate. Direction: if months are positively dependent, the true intervals are wider than reported.

### Added after the audit (mentor)

- **Data and risk-free rate.** Prices come from one vendor, and adjusted close is re-based whenever a dividend goes ex (a later download put JPM's levels 0.4965% lower by a constant, with returns agreeing to below 1e-6). The monthly risk-free rate is TB3MS ÷ 100 ÷ 12, with three approximations (see the table). Dropping the rate altogether raises the Sharpe levels by about 0.15 and moves the gaps by at most 0.016.
- **Process.** The lock time (12 October 2026, 13:32 SGT) is self-reported: a Git timestamp cannot show that it predates the first run. The rule that no 2024–2026 stock-return or Sharpe figure was shown before the lock is also self-reported.
- **Comparison with the paper.** The draft's Sharpe ratios are monthly, ours are annualised; its samples, assets (3 to 24), risk-free series, test lengths and P-value conventions differ, and its Table 3 is M = 120 only. Patterns can be compared, levels cannot.

## Part 3 — The three that matter most (mentor's ranking)

Criterion: an assumption matters most when its failure could move the reading and no check in the project bounds it. Bounded by a check, so not on the list: independence of months (block SEs 0.74 to 1.02 times the locked formula SEs), the risk-free approximations (gaps move by at most 0.016 even with the rate dropped), the solver (7.1), look-ahead (7.3), the choice of 1/N version (+0.010 [−0.165, +0.184]) and costs (at most about 0.014 at 25 basis points; mentor arithmetic). If the months were dependent the intervals would be too narrow, which cannot turn "no win shown" into a win. The window lengths are not on the list either: M = 60 and M = 120 are named in the question (RQ v3.1), the two results differ by +0.101 [−0.091, +0.292], and other lengths fall under item 3.

1. **The single 105-month path.** The largest movement measured anywhere in the project: the gaps against 1/N change sign between 2018–2023 and 2024–2026 (B − A −0.776 and −0.726), and 33 months cannot tell noise from change (smallest detectable B − A 1.147 and 1.306).
2. **Hindsight in the choice of stocks.** It cannot be tested inside the design. It probably makes the reported gap too high, so it cannot be hiding a win, but it could be the reason for the small positive gaps (+0.005, +0.106).
3. **The reach of the result.** Four stocks, one plug-in rule and two window lengths (60 and 120 months). Few assets is the optimiser's favourable case (p. 3), and the draft says longer windows favour it too (p. 3), so other window lengths could differ from these two. The reading does not carry to larger universes, to other rules or to other window lengths.
